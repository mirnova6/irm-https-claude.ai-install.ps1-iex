Our Heritage

Rooted in Legend.
Refined for Now.

Afsane was born from a question: what would it mean to dress in a way that honors where you come from? We are a label shaped by the Persian literary tradition — by the Shahnameh's heroes, by Hafez's verse, by the geometric poetry of Isfahan's tiles.

Every piece we create carries an intention. The Ardashir Coat was named for a king who understood that power is worn, not declared. The Simurgh Blazer takes its form from the mythical bird of paradise — structured yet free, ancient yet forward.

We don't make costume. We make clothing for people who carry their history with grace.

About

Our Beginning

The Story
Behind the Name

Afsane began with a simple refusal: the refusal to dress as if your history doesn't matter. Founded in 2022 by a team of designers with roots in Tehran and educations in Milan and London, Afsane was conceived as a response — not to a trend, but to a silence.

Persian culture has shaped human civilization for over two thousand years. Its poetry, its architecture, its textile traditions, its philosophy of beauty — all of this exists in the world. Yet the fashion industry largely pretends it doesn't. Afsane exists to end that silence, and to do it elegantly.

Our name says everything. Afsane — افسانه — means legend, fable, myth. It is the word Persian parents use to begin a story that they want their children to carry forever. We make clothing for those people. The ones who carry stories worth telling.

Tehran · Milan · London

The Name

افسانه

Afsane · af·sā·ne · Persian noun

Legend. Fable. Myth.

In Persian, afsane is the word for the stories that live longer than the people who first told them. It is used for Shahnameh heroes and ancient fables alike. It carries within it a sense of something that was never quite of this world — something that transcends time.

We chose this name because we believe clothing can function the same way. The best things you wear become part of your story. They accumulate meaning. They outlast you. That is what we build toward, one piece at a time.

The Foundations

What We Draw From

I

The Shahnameh

Ferdowsi's 60,000-verse epic poem, completed in 1010 CE, is the longest poem ever written by a single author. It tells the history of Persia through its heroes, kings, and myths — and it remains the spiritual backbone of Persian identity. We name our pieces after its characters not as decoration, but as declaration.

II

The Simurgh

The Simurgh is the great mythical bird of Persian legend — ancient, all-knowing, perched on the World Tree at the center of the universe. It appears in the Shahnameh as the protector of heroes and the symbol of divine wisdom. We use the Simurgh as our emblem of the eternal: something that carries the past but is never trapped by it.

III

The Persian Garden

The Persian garden — the pairidaeza, from which we get the word "paradise" — is a philosophy of ordered beauty. Four rivers, four quadrants, an enclosed world of deliberate harmony. It is the model for our collections: structured, purposeful, beautiful in a way that requires restraint. Every collection is a garden.

Our Mission

"Fashion as a form
of cultural memory."

We believe that what you wear is never neutral. It communicates identity, inheritance, aspiration. Our mission is to give people — particularly those from the Persian diaspora and those who love what Persia has given the world — clothing that is worthy of that communication. Premium in quality. Thoughtful in reference. Beautiful without apology.

How We Make

Craftsmanship
Without Compromise

Every Afsane piece is produced in small batches, working with atelier partners in Istanbul and Milan who share our standards for construction and ethics. We do not produce more than we can stand behind.

Our fabrics are sourced from mills in northern Italy and the British Isles — wool, cashmere, silk, and organic cotton that have been built to last far beyond a single season. We are allergic to disposability.

We limit production by design. Not to manufacture scarcity, but because we believe quality and scale are, in the end, incompatible. When a piece is gone, it is gone. That is not a marketing decision. That is a values decision.
